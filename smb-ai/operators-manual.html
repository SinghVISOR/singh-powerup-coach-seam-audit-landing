Halt

Put a deliberate gap between the impulse and the tool

A machine that never has to breathe will happily talk you out of ever breathing again. Its readiness is contagious — it pulls you into its tempo until you're making founder decisions at machine speed. The gap is where you stay the operator instead of the operated.

Operator moveRun the Operator's Check (60s) at the top of every Cowork session: What state am I in? What am I actually trying to produce? What would the wisest version of me reach for? No session brief, no build.

Understand the Real Question

A search-box question gets a search-box answer

The difference between a beige generic slab and something sharp enough to ship is the four minutes you spend framing the real decision and handing Claude its non-negotiables — before the prompt, not after.

Operator moveWrite "What I'm actually trying to decide is ______," then prime the session with a God Mode block (below) so context and non-negotiables are set once, not rebuilt every time.
M

Mark the Line

Draw the line between machine work and human work

Give Claude the drafting, the code, the aggregation, the repetition. Keep the judgment, the client relationship, the values call, the final word. Draw it on purpose — the approval queue is this line made into software, and you built it that way for a reason.

Operator moveFor every build or send, name it: "This goes to Claude / this stays with me." If a task has no human side, look harder — that's usually the part that mattered.
A

Apply the Leverage

Once the line is drawn, use the machine boldly on its side

The quiet failure for a solo founder is preciousness — using a supercomputer like a notepad. Hand Claude the whole machine side: every Vercel function, every SQL query, every OAuth handler, every draft. Delegate fully so you can invest deeply in the two things only you can do — the thinking and the relationships.

Operator moveThis is the whole no-hire engine below. Delegate completely, approve deliberately.
N

Never Outsource the Core

If a machine can generate it, a machine can copy it

Your judgment, your values, the final call, your purpose. It gets outsourced not in one dramatic moment but in a hundred small Tuesdays where the model's confidence is more convenient than your responsibility. The names and clever solutions a machine generates, you can't own — and neither can anyone else. The moat is you.

Operator moveKeep the "never outsource" short list pinned. Any consequential write clears the approval gate with you reading the exact content — never a summary.
Apply the Leverage · in practice

The no-hire engine

You already have your engineering team: Claude, in this Cowork session. You act as product manager; Claude writes every Vercel function, Supabase query, and OAuth handler; you test and approve. Here's exactly how a new skill ships without an engineer.

1

Design the skill

Spec, system prompt, example inputs/outputs — iterate in a Claude Project until the output is right. Judgment step; stays with you.

2

Claude writes the Vercel function

Ask Claude to write the serverless function for the skill endpoint. Keys stay server-side, never in the frontend.

3

Claude writes the React skill card

The card calls the endpoint and shows an approve / reject control — the Mark-the-Line UI.

4

Store as pending_approval

Result lands in Supabase with status = pending_approval; the approval queue polls for pending items.

5

Approve → the write fires

On your approval, Supabase fires the Make.com webhook, which performs the real write action (send, post, invoice).

Keep a Claude Project called "Dev assistant": paste errors, get fixes, generate boilerplate. Do not hire until ~$2k MRR — and the first hire's only job is to harden what already works.

Dogfood SOPs — the six areas

You run SMB.AI's own operations on SMB.AI. Same six areas as the Owner's Playbook, here as tight internal SOPs with a service level and the one metric you hold yourself to.

Area 1

Customers & Sales

SLA: every lead touched in 24h

SOP: Daily 10-min triage — Claude pulls new signups/leads, enriches, and drafts first-touch. You decide who's worth a call and make it sound like you. Never outsource: the actual relationship.

Area 2

Money & Finance

SLA: days-to-payment < 20

SOP: Weekly — Claude lists overdue, drafts chases, queues them; you approve and soften the two that matter. Monthly — cash-flow snapshot + close. Never outsource: the numbers you sign off.

Area 3

Comms & Inbox

SLA: no reply older than 24h

SOP: Twice-daily triage; pre-meeting brief before every external call. Never outsource: any message where the relationship is the point (David's rule).

Area 4

Marketing & Content

SLA: ship what you planned

SOP: Weekly batch — Claude turns one idea into a week of posts + newsletter in your voice; you pick the two with a real point of view (Renata's leverage). Never outsource: the opinion.

Area 5

Data & Reporting

SLA: report acted on, not filed

SOP: Automated weekly competitor scan (cron) + monthly report on the 1st. Claude gathers; you interpret and decide. Never outsource: what the numbers mean.

Area 6

Admin & Compliance

SLA: nothing slips

SOP: Contract review on demand; renewals and RLS/privacy obligations tracked. Claude reads and flags; you sign. Never outsource: the signature and the risk you accept. AI analysis, not legal advice.

Core frameworks

Military · Handoff

SMEAC

Operations order across five dimensions: Situation · Mission · Execution · Admin/Logistics · Command/Signal. Nothing left open.

In ops: spec any skill build or dev/offshore handoff.

Structure this build using SMEAC (Situation, Mission, Execution, Admin/Logistics, Command/Signal). Audience: the dev workstream. Flag every open question as [OPEN QUESTION]; list dependencies explicitly.
Military · Decide fast

OODA

Observe → Orient → Decide → Act → repeat. A speed-of-decision cycle, not an analysis format.

In ops: a competitor moves, an outage hits, a deal wobbles.

Apply OODA to [scenario]. Move fast — identify the decision I need to make now, not just the data.
Military · Report

BLUF

Bottom Line Up Front. Conclusion first, rationale after. Kills the buried lede.

In ops: every status update, cash-flow snapshot, investor note.

Respond in BLUF format. Give me the recommendation in the first sentence, then the supporting logic.
Business · Persuade

SCQA

Situation → Complication → Question → Answer. The backbone of persuasive executive communication.

In ops: proposals, the pitch, partner & board updates.

Write this using SCQA (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer). Audience: prospective investors. Tone: authoritative and warm.
Business · Prioritize

RICE

Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort. Cuts through opinion with a defensible numeric ranking.

In ops: which skill to build next; rank the roadmap.

Score these options using RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort) and rank them. Show your working.
Logic · Check

MECE

Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive. A quality check on any breakdown — no overlaps, no gaps.

In ops: sanity-check any list or plan before you act.

Review this breakdown and ensure it is MECE. Flag any overlaps or gaps.
Intel · Analyze

PMESII-PT

Political, Military, Economic, Social, Infrastructure, Information, Physical Terrain. Full-spectrum situational analysis.

In ops: market entry, competitive landscape, risk assessment.

Run a PMESII-PT analysis on [market]. Use MECE to structure each dimension. Add confidence ratings.

Guardrails — hold the line

From the Master Handover. These apply at every stage and don't bend for convenience — that's how the core gets outsourced.

Never expose keys in the frontend. Claude and Supabase keys route through serverless functions only.
Never store tokens as plaintext. Encrypt OAuth tokens at rest (AES-256); never commit .env.
Every write/send needs human approval. The card shows exact content, not a summary. If Make.com fails, warn — never silently drop.
Verify CRON_SECRET on every cron endpoint before it runs.
Enable Supabase RLS before more than one customer has access.
Legal/financial outputs carry a disclaimer. Contract review: "AI analysis, not legal advice."
Keep claude-sonnet-4-6. Don't switch models to save money.
