The HUMAN Framework — the Conscious Operator's discipline

Your Prompt Mastery Playbook is full of frameworks for thinking and prompting — SMEAC, OODA, BLUF, SCQA. HUMAN is the missing counterpart: five moves that keep a person conscious and in command while working at machine speed. Not a prompt trick — the discipline of the Conscious Operator versus the Reactive one. Each letter is a practice you can do today.

H

Halt

Put a deliberate gap between the impulse and the tool — before you open the tab, before you fire the prompt. A gap just long enough for the human to catch up to the machine. A machine that never has to breathe will happily talk you out of ever breathing again.

Practice: before your next AI tool, take 60 seconds and write one line you'll never show anyone — "Right now I am ______, and what I actually want from this is ______." No line, no tool.
In the platform → the conscious pause before you run a skill
U

Understand the Real Question

A search-box question gets a search-box answer, no matter how brilliant the machine. Give it who the customer is, what you've tried, and its non-negotiables ("never assume a budget we don't have; if unsure, say so"). First, name the honest decision underneath.

Practice: before your next real prompt, write one sentence — "What I'm actually trying to decide is ______." Then, and only then, give the machine the context and the non-negotiables.
In the platform → a well-framed brief (uses God Mode / SMEAC from your toolkit)
M

Mark the Line

Every task has a line. On one side, the work for the machine — summarizing, drafting, aggregating, the tireless repetition. On the other, the work that must stay human — the judgment, the relationship, the values call. The Conscious Operator draws it on purpose, every time; the Reactive one lets the machine erase it.

Practice: take one task today and draw the line out loud — "This part goes to the machine. This part stays with me." If you can't find a part that stays with you, look harder; that's usually the part that mattered.
In the platform → read-only runs vs. write/send that queues for approval
A

Apply the Leverage

Once the line is drawn, use the machine boldly on its side. The quiet failure is timidly asking a supercomputer to fix a typo. Delegate the machine's work fully so you can invest the human's work deeply — leverage on one side buys presence on the other.

Practice: name one thing you still do by hand that lives entirely on the machine's side of the line — and this week, hand it over completely. Then spend the time it gives back on the one thing only you can do.
In the platform → run the 31 skills fully on the machine's side
N

Never Outsource the Core

A small, non-negotiable core never goes to a machine: your judgment, your values, the final call, your purpose. It gets outsourced not in one dramatic moment but in a hundred small Tuesdays where the machine's confidence is more convenient than your responsibility. If a machine can generate it, a machine can copy it — what can't be copied is you.

Practice: write your short list — judgment, values, the final call, purpose — the things you'll never outsource no matter how convenient it becomes. Keep it where you'll see it. The day the machine offers to take one off your hands won't feel like a threat; it'll feel like a favor.
In the platform → the approval gate: you hold the final call
The Operator's Check — 60 seconds before any AI tool (the "H") 1. What state am I in right now? 2. What am I actually trying to produce? 3. If the wisest version of me were sitting here, what would they want me to reach for?
Why it's the center of the playbook: HUMAN reframes AI not as "more tools, faster" but as a discipline of conscious command. It also lands perfectly on the product — SMB.AI's approval queue is Mark the Line and Never Outsource built into software. In the full build, each letter carries a worked story (Priya, Jamal, David, Renata, Marcus) to make it stick.

The core spine — shared by all four flavors

Every flavor is built on the same backbone: the HUMAN operating framework, six operations areas, and a repeatable per-area template. Only the framing and a few audience-specific sections change between flavors.

1 · The HUMAN Framework (the operating model)

The Conscious Operator's discipline — the five moves that keep a person in command while the machine does the work. Your Master Handover's approval model is where two of these letters (Mark the Line, Never Outsource) become software. Full detail on the HUMAN Framework tab.

H · Halt→ U · Understand→ M · Mark the Line→ A · Apply Leverage→ N · Never Outsource
  • Read-only tasks run instantly (research, lookups, summaries); write / send tasks always queue for approval.
  • The approval card shows exact content that will be sent — never a summary.
  • Guardrails carry through: keys server-side only, tokens encrypted, legal/financial disclaimers, human-in-the-loop always.

3 · The per-area template (every area runs the HUMAN loop)

Each of the six areas follows this exact shape

  1. The job to be done — the outcome an owner actually wants.
  2. The HUMAN discipline for this task — Halt, Understand the real question, Mark the line, Apply the leverage, Never outsource the core — made concrete for the area.
  3. Skills & connectors — which SMB.AI skills and integrations power it.
  4. Where you Mark the Line — what goes to the machine, what stays human, and what to check before the write/send fires.
  5. Cadence — daily / weekly / monthly rhythm so it becomes habit.
  6. The metric that proves it works — e.g. days-to-payment, inbox-zero time, leads contacted.
  7. Copy-paste prompts — 2–3 ready starters, matching your toolkit's template style.

4 · Cross-cutting sections (wrap the whole playbook)

  • Getting started — crawl / walk / run: a 30-day ramp so an owner isn't overwhelmed.
  • The weekly & monthly operating rhythm: one calendar view of every recurring AI task.
  • Your operations scoreboard: the handful of metrics that show AI is actually paying off.
  • Trust, safety & guardrails: Mark the Line and Never Outsource the Core, plus the security rules, in plain language.
